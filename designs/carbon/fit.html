How it works.

Three short parts, one question at a time.

  1. Tell us about your riding.

    What you ride for, how far you usually go, and anything that hurts or goes numb.

  2. Measure yourself.

    Height, inside leg and a quick flexibility check. Each step shows you how.

  3. Measure your bike.

    Optional. Add how your bike is set up now, and we’ll show which settings sit inside or outside your starting range.

You’ll need: a tape measure, a hardback book, a wall, your bike and, ideally, a friend to help.

Why such small steps?

Experienced fitters adjust a bike a few millimetres at a time, then test it. Here are some of the numbers they work with, on a ruler enlarged to fill your screen.

  • 2–3 mm

    The most saddle change to try yourself after a professional fit.

    Mat Steinmetz, 51 Speedshop2

  • 3 mm

    The step size for finding saddle height, testing each change under load.

    Steve Hogg, fitter1

  • 5–10 mm

    The most front-end change to try yourself after a professional fit.

    Mat Steinmetz, 51 Speedshop2

  • 10 mm

    Where cleats start: the pedal axle at least this far behind the ball of the foot.

    Jimmy Wilson, Cyclefit3

Before you change anything.

A few habits keep every adjustment safe and easy to undo.

  • Mark where you are now.Measure and write down each setting before you touch it.
  • Change one thing at a time.Then you’ll know which change made the difference.
  • Use the right torque.Follow the setting printed on each part, especially with carbon.
  • Respect the limits.Keep the seatpost above its minimum-insertion mark, and stay within the fork maker’s limit for spacers.
  • Test on a short ride first.Don’t make a big change just before a long ride.
  • Get help if it hurts.Pain or numbness that doesn’t settle means seeing a bike fitter, a physio or your GP.
